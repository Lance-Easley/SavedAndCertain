Free Grace Theology is False

An examination of Free Grace Theology — the view that saving faith requires no ongoing commitment — and why Scripture teaches otherwise.

Why Am I Writing This?

I came across Free Grace Theology online, through many debates and some comments. I understand now that it is a natural interpretation of “once saved, always saved”, emphasizing “once” as numerically. Because of the many verses in Scripture that warn of apostasy and highlight that it is an enduring faith that truly saves, I’ve changed how I say that phrase: “if saved, always saved”. Ultimately, I want the Biblical perspective that takes into account all of Scripture in a non-contradictory way.

What Am I Not Saying?

I am NOT saying that eternal security is false. On the contrary, I’ve written an apologetic writing supporting it. What I point out in that writing though is that those who truly believe will be sustained and guarded by God through their faith. Free Grace Theology would claim that this sustaining/perseverance is irrelevant regarding one’s salvation.

Summary

There’s a common non-sequitur with Free Grace Theologists that we see here: ‘if God is the active agent who keeps/guards/preserves us and our inheritance, then there is no necessity of faith on our part.’ What this view misses is the fact that God guards and keeps us through our faith. Faith is granted/authored by God and we are preserved in our faith by God.

  • Acts 11:18 - When they heard these things they fell silent. And they glorified God, saying, "Then to the Gentiles also God has granted repentance that leads to life."
  • Acts 13:47-48 - For so the Lord has commanded us, saying,
    "'I have made you a light for the Gentiles,
    that you may bring salvation to the ends of the earth.'"
    And when the Gentiles heard this, they began rejoicing and glorifying the word of the Lord, and as many as were appointed to eternal life believed.
  • Acts 16:14 - One who heard us was a woman named Lydia, from the city of Thyatira, a seller of purple goods, who was a worshiper of God. The Lord opened her heart to pay attention to what was said by Paul.
  • 1 Corinthians 1:30 - And because of him you are in Christ Jesus, who became to us wisdom from God, righteousness and sanctification and redemption,
  • Philippians 1:28-29 - and not frightened in anything by your opponents. This is a clear sign to them of their destruction, but of your salvation, and that from God. For it has been granted to you that for the sake of Christ you should not only believe in him but also suffer for his sake,
  • 2 Timothy 2:25 correcting his opponents with gentleness. God may perhaps grant them repentance leading to a knowledge of the truth,
  • Hebrews 12:2 - looking to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith, who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross, despising the shame, and is seated at the right hand of the throne of God.
  • 1 Peter 1:4-5 - to an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you, who by God’s power are being guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.

Because sustained faith is a sign of God’s election and preservation, there are many verses that legitimately emphasize sustained faith as saving.

  • Matthew 10:22 - “…But the one who endures to the end will be saved.”
  • Matthew 24:13 - “But the one who endures to the end will be saved.”
  • Mark 13:13 - “…But the one who endures to the end will be saved.”
  • John 15:6 - “If anyone does not remain in Me, he is like a branch that is thrown away and withers. Such branches are gathered up, thrown into the fire, and burned.”
  • 1 Corinthians 15:2 - and by which you are being saved, if you hold fast to the word I preached to you—unless you believed in vain.
  • Colossians 1:22–23 - he has now reconciled in his body of flesh by his death, in order to present you holy and blameless and above reproach before him, if indeed you continue in the faith, stable and steadfast, not shifting from the hope of the gospel that you heard, which has been proclaimed in all creation under heaven, and of which I, Paul, became a minister.
  • Hebrews 3:12–14 - Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God. But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called “today,” that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin. For we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end.
  • Hebrews 10:35–39 - Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised. For,
    “Yet a little while,
    and the coming one will come and will not delay;
    but my righteous one shall live by faith,
    and if he shrinks back,
    my soul has no pleasure in him.”

    But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls.

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life.”

  • The Greek word for believes is πιστεύων, which is a verb in the “present participle active” sense.
  • I agree with the opposition’s claim here, but differ slightly with the last statement. It is true that this specific verse does not speak of a condition of keeping faith, other than the present active tense of “believes”. Other verses do expand on this perseverance condition though, that is where the disagreement would happen, not here. Such verses can be found in the verses supporting section.

  • Opposition’s claim: Stated as a certainty, not a conditional hope.
  • Context:
    • 2 Timothy 4:7-8 - I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith. Henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will award to me on that day, and not only to me but also to all who have loved his appearing.
    • 2 Timothy 4:17 - But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me, so that through me the message might be fully proclaimed and all the Gentiles might hear it. So I was rescued from the lion’s mouth.
  • Paul had explained in verses 7-8 that he kept the faith, so henceforth the Lord will award him the crown of righteousness on that day. Does that mean that it was Paul’s strength that got him there? No. It is a common theme throughout the verses brought up here that God is the one who preserves our faith. We even see this with the verse in question, given the prior verse: “the Lord stood by me and strengthened me”. We still have certainty, as though enduring faith is required, that faith is God’s working in us.

Consequently, he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them.

Hebrews 7:25

  • Opposition’s claim: Security rests on Christ's ongoing intercessory work, not the believer's perseverance
  • Context:
    • Hebrews 7:23-24 - The former priests were many in number, because they were prevented by death from continuing in office, but he holds his priesthood permanently, because he continues forever.
    • Hebrews 7:26-28 - For it was indeed fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, unstained, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens. He has no need, like those high priests, to offer sacrifices daily, first for his own sins and then for those of the people, since he did this once for all when he offered up himself. For the law appoints men in their weakness as high priests, but the word of the oath, which came later than the law, appoints a Son who has been made perfect forever.
    • Hebrews 3:12–14 - Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God. But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called “today,” that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin. For we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end.
    • Hebrews 10:32–39 - But recall the former days when, after you were enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings, sometimes being publicly exposed to reproach and affliction, and sometimes being partners with those so treated. For you had compassion on those in prison, and you joyfully accepted the plundering of your property, since you knew that you yourselves had a better possession and an abiding one. Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised. For,
      “Yet a little while,
      and the coming one will come and will not delay;
      but my righteous one shall live by faith,
       and if he shrinks back,
       my soul has no pleasure in him.”

      But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls.
  • In Hebrews 7, the context is not focused on the individual, but on Christ’s priesthood. The permanence described in the passage is attributed to Christ.
  • I agree that our security rests on Christ’s ongoing intercessory work, but that does not eliminate the necessity of preserving faith. The context of Hebrews is clear that we have need of enduring confidence/faith, otherwise we did not share in Christ to begin with.

Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, “I will never leave you nor forsake you.”

Hebrews 13:5

  • Opposition’s claim: An unconditional promise of God's continued presence, with no stated condition of continued faith attached.
  • Context:
    • Hebrews 13:6-9 - So we can confidently say,
       “The Lord is my helper;
       I will not fear;
      what can man do to me?”
      Remember your leaders, those who spoke to you the word of God. Consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith. Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever. Do not be led away by diverse and strange teachings, for it is good for the heart to be strengthened by grace, not by foods, which have not benefited those devoted to them.
    • Hebrews 3:12–14 - Take care, brothers, lest there be in any of you an evil, unbelieving heart, leading you to fall away from the living God. But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called “today,” that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin. For we have come to share in Christ, if indeed we hold our original confidence firm to the end.
    • Hebrews 10:32–39 - But recall the former days when, after you were enlightened, you endured a hard struggle with sufferings, sometimes being publicly exposed to reproach and affliction, and sometimes being partners with those so treated. For you had compassion on those in prison, and you joyfully accepted the plundering of your property, since you knew that you yourselves had a better possession and an abiding one. Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God you may receive what is promised. For,
      “Yet a little while,
      and the coming one will come and will not delay;
      but my righteous one shall live by faith,
       and if he shrinks back,
       my soul has no pleasure in him.”

      But we are not of those who shrink back and are destroyed, but of those who have faith and preserve their souls.
    • Hebrews 12:25 - See that you do not refuse him who is speaking. For if they did not escape when they refused him who warned them on earth, much less will we escape if we reject him who warns from heaven.
  • The stated condition of continued faith is implied by the next verses, where we can say “The Lord is my helper; I will not fear; what can man do to me?”, and we are encouraged to imitate the faith of those who spoke the word of God to us. The rest of Hebrews also explicitly states this necessity of the perseverance of faith, as shown in the context above.

I write these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God, that you may know that you have eternal life.

1 John 5:13

  • Opposition’s claim: The stated purpose of the letter is so believers "may know" (present, settled certainty) they have eternal life — not "may know, provided they keep believing."
  • The Greek word used for believe is πιστεύουσιν, which is in the present participle active tense.
  • Context:
    • 1 John 5:4-5 - For everyone who has been born of God overcomes the world. And this is the victory that has overcome the world—our faith. Who is it that overcomes the world except the one who believes that Jesus is the Son of God?
  • The verse itself gives a condition for this knowing, “to you who believe” (present active tense). It does not say ‘to you who believed’ (past tense).

Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ! According to his great mercy, he has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead, to an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, kept in heaven for you, who by God’s power are being guarded through faith for a salvation ready to be revealed in the last time.

1 Peter 1:3–5

  • Opposition’s claim: Believers are "kept by the power of God," not by their own power to keep believing — the guarding agent is God, not the human will.
  • The opposition stops early in the verse. It is true that we are kept by the power of God, and He is the guarding agent, but the verse continues by saying “who by God’s power are being guarded through faith”. God guards our inheritance of Heaven through our faith.

Now to him who is able to keep you from stumbling and to present you blameless before the presence of his glory with great joy,

Jude 24

  • Opposition’s claim: God is described as "able to keep you from stumbling" and present you blameless — an ability-based promise, not a conditional one.
  • Context:
    • Jude 3-6 - Beloved, although I was very eager to write to you about our common salvation, I found it necessary to write appealing to you to contend for the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints. For certain people have crept in unnoticed who long ago were designated for this condemnation, ungodly people, who pervert the grace of our God into sensuality and deny our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ.
      Now I want to remind you, although you once fully knew it, that Jesus, who saved a people out of the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who did not believe. And the angels who did not stay within their own position of authority, but left their proper dwelling, he has kept in eternal chains under gloomy darkness until the judgment of the great day—
    • Jude 20-23 - But you, beloved, building yourselves up in your most holy faith and praying in the Holy Spirit, keep yourselves in the love of God, waiting for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ that leads to eternal life. And have mercy on those who doubt; save others by snatching them out of the fire; to others show mercy with fear, hating even the garment stained by the flesh.
  • Jude 3-4 describes a designated condemnation for certain people who “pervert the grace of our God into sensuality and deny our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ.” He continues with the example of Jesus destroying the ones who He delivered from Egypt who afterward did not believe, and the angels who did not stay in within their own position were kept in eternal chains until Judgement Day. These are both typological inferences to apostasy, and the outcome of losing faith was not Heaven, but Hell.
  • What is meant by “keep you from stumbling”? Given the verses just prior, I’d argue that this references our faith. Verse 20 is an exhortation to build ourselves up in our faith and prayer, keeping ourselves in the love of God. Verse 23 tells us to “save others by snatching them out of the fire”, which both sides should agree means preaching the gospel to them and them believing/trusting in Jesus. This build up of encouraging us to build up in faith and to snatch others out of the fire leads right into verse 24’s “keep you from stumbling”. If that phrase refers to us not stumbling in faith, then that lines up with the other verses describing God guarding/perfecting our faith.
  • I actually agree with the opposition’s claim, God is able to keep us from stumbling and will present us blameless before Him. This does not however eliminate the necessity of faith. As shown above, Jude reinforces the condemnation of apostasy, but that does not tamper with God’s promise since other verses show that our faith is God’s guarding of our salvation.

How To Change My Mind

As I mentioned in the beginning of this document, I want the Biblical perspective on this. In order to convince me that I’m wrong on this topic, I’d need the following (in order of significance)

  1. An explanation of all (or at least most) of the verses in support by
    • Using the context of the verse and the rest of the Bible
    • Bringing up the Greek/Hebrew word
    • Then from either/both of those, make your case
  2. A refutation of my explanations of the verses against. I’ve used a similar framework that I just listed to explain these verses.
