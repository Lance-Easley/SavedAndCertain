Was Paul Confident or Certain
He Would Go To Heaven?

Examining Paul's own words to see whether he expressed mere confidence or full certainty about his place in Heaven.

Why am I writing this?

I’ve heard different perspectives on whether Paul himself was confident that he was saved and is going to Heaven, and I want to clear this up. Ultimately I want to know what the Biblical perspective is.

This is considered a “sister document” to my Eternal Security document, as this narrows down the eternal security doctrine to just Paul and his writings.

What Am I Not Saying?

Paul’s confidence does not negate his warnings against sin, his encouragement to do good works, nor the necessity of perseverance. Paul grounds his, and all other Christians’, justification and the believer’s perseverance in God’s work, grace, and power, rather than his own strength and will to merit such.

Summary

Paul speaks of his confidence in salvation many times and in many ways:

  • “Who shall bring any charge against God’s elect? It is God who justifies. Who is to condemn? …Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? …nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.”
    - Romans 8:32-39
  • “our Lord Jesus Christ, who will sustain you to the end, guiltless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.”
    - 1 Corinthians 1:7-8
  • “we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. …He who has prepared us for this very thing is God, who has given us the Spirit as a guarantee.”
    - 2 Corinthians 5:1-5
  • “and believed in him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit, who is the guarantee of our inheritance until we acquire possession of it”
    - Ephesians 1:13-14
  • “may your whole spirit and soul and body be kept blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. He who calls you is faithful; he will surely do it.”
    - 1 Thessalonians 5:23-24
  • “I know whom I have believed, and I am convinced that he is able to guard until that day what has been entrusted to me. …By the Holy Spirit who dwells within us, guard the good deposit entrusted to you.”
    - 2 Timothy 1:12-14
  • “The Lord will rescue me from every evil deed and bring me safely into his heavenly kingdom.”
    - 2 Timothy 4:18

The verses that are typically used against this view are taken out of their context and applied to Paul’s justification before God. A common theme is taking verses related to Paul’s ministry work, the future resurrection, and Paul’s encouragement against sinning, and applying them to Paul’s present and enduring justification. Each verse is broken down below, where I demonstrate this context and address the opposing claim.

Paul clearly and consistently communicates that he would be guarded by God’s power and kept blameless before God to the end, thus being confident that the Lord will bring him into His Heavenly Kingdom. At the same time, Paul strives in his ministry work and encourages others to do the same. Paul does not consider his own works meritorious to salvation, rather that it is God who justified him and will keep him.

Verses In Support of This View

All verses are from the ESV translation. Where helpful, we go back to the Greek.

For I am sure that neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers, nor height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.

Romans 8:38-39

  • Context:
    • Romans 8:29-30 - For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, in order that he might be the firstborn among many brothers. And those whom he predestined he also called, and those whom he called he also justified, and those whom he justified he also glorified.
    • Romans 8:33-35 - Who shall bring any charge against God’s elect? It is God who justifies. Who is to condemn? Christ Jesus is the one who died—more than that, who was raised—who is at the right hand of God, who indeed is interceding for us. Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or danger, or sword?
  • Paul anchors what he means by “the love of God/Christ”, being in reference to justification. This is right on the heels of what’s called “the golden chain” in verse 30, those whom He predestined…He also glorified. With that, Paul can reasonably argue that nothing in all creation could separate us from Christ or make the predestined lose justification/prevent glorification.
  • Romans 8:30 leaves no room for any condition on Paul or our part. If God predestined, then he will justify and glorify. There is no category of “predestined, justified, but not glorified”.
  • Since it is God who justified Paul, Christ who intercedes for him, and thus no charge can be brought against Paul, whether things present or things to come, to say Paul could’ve lost his salvation would require one of three things to happen:
    1. God reversing justification (contradicts 8:33)
    1. Christ failing in intercession, present or future (contradicts 8:34, 38 respectively)
    1. The golden chain breaking (contradicts 8:30)

  • At surface level, this verse may sound like we have to “sow” to reap eternal life for ourselves. However the immediate context tells us how we should interpret the parable, as an encouragement to serve one another, not “unless you serve one another you will not be saved”. The warnings here serve as a real contrast (flesh leads to destruction, Spirit leads to life), but Paul consistently links being in Christ to the Spirit and Christ’s work, not Paul or the believer’s own meritorious works.
    • Paul just described that though we are free in Christ, we should not use this freedom to satisfy the flesh. Paul repeats this encouragement multiple times throughout his letters, as such good works will bring glory to God through Jesus Christ. These good works are not described as the means by which we ourselves merit eternal life, otherwise we’d have a reason to boast—Ephesians 2:8-10

    • Therefore, viewing the phrase “the one who sows to the Spirit will from the Spirit reap eternal life” in it’s context, Paul is describing eternal life as the end-times harvest of Spirit-led living. This does not mean eternal life is merited by works, but that persevering in the Spirit is the intended trajectory of those who belong to Christ.

This is why Paul was confident that God would bring him safely into God’s heavenly kingdom, being given the Holy Spirit as the guarantee, sustaining him to the end, guiltless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ. (See verses in support)
    1. What Paul has not yet attained is “the resurrection from the dead”, which is what will happen in the future—whether alive or dead—to those who are saved. The next verse answers that to my point: “who will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious body”

How To Change My Mind

As I mentioned in the beginning of this document, I want the Biblical perspective on this. In order to convince me that I’m wrong on this topic, I’d need the following (in order of significance)

  1. An explanation of all (or at least most) of the verses in support by
    • Using the context of the verse and the rest of the Bible
    • Bringing up the Greek/Hebrew word
    • Then from either/both of those, make your case
  2. A refutation of my explanations of the verses against. I’ve used a similar framework that I just listed to explain these verses.
